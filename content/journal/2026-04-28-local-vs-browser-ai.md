Most organizations running AI tools right now are doing it through a browser. Someone opens ChatGPT or Claude, types a question, gets an answer. That workflow is fast, accessible, and carries almost no setup cost. It is also routing organizational information through infrastructure the organization does not control, under terms of service most staff have never read.

This is not a catastrophe. It is a governance gap, and it is one that most organizations are going to have to address.

The alternative is a local model: an open-weight AI that runs inside your own infrastructure rather than on a commercial platform. The technology is the same generation as the tools your staff are already using. What changes is where the computation happens and who has custody of the data.

## What "local" actually means

A local AI model is a language model you download and run on your own servers or private cloud rather than accessing through a vendor's API. Models like Llama 3, Mistral, and Gemma are publicly available, production-quality, and capable of the same tasks your staff are using consumer tools for today. Drafting, summarizing, analyzing documents, answering questions about internal records.

The difference is not capability. It is accountability.

When a staff member queries a local model, the prompt stays inside the system boundary. The response stays inside the system boundary. There is no data leaving to train a commercial platform. There is no vendor retaining records of what your organization asked. There is a log you control, under policies you set.

## When it makes sense

Local AI makes particular sense for organizations that handle information with legal or regulatory sensitivity. Healthcare associations and professional bodies operating under PIPEDA or PIPA. Organizations whose members share sensitive information as part of the service relationship. Organizations that need to answer clearly when a board member asks: what does your AI system have access to, and where does that information go?

If the honest answer to that question is "we use ChatGPT and we are not entirely sure," that is a conversation worth having before it becomes a larger one.

## When it does not yet make sense

Local models require infrastructure to run on. For a small organization without a technical staff member or a managed IT relationship, the setup costs can outweigh the governance benefit, at least at first. A staff of five people using consumer tools carefully, with a clear policy, may be in a more defensible position than a staff of five people running a poorly configured local environment.

The goal is not local for its own sake. The goal is governed. Sometimes those are the same thing. Sometimes the path to governed runs through better consumer tool policies rather than a local deployment.

Getting that diagnosis right is the work.
